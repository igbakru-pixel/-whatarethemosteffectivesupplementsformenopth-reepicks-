<h2><strong>Vitamin D: the best supplement for vitamin deficiency checks</strong></h2>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Men often ask what vitamins should I take, and vitamin D is one of the few where a blood test can give a straight answer.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is a top pick for men who get little sun, live far from the equator, or have darker skin or a mostly indoor job.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes that</span><a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/vitamind-consumer/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">vitamin D</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> helps the body absorb calcium and supports bone health.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is one of the best supplements for vitamin deficiency, but only if you actually have a shortfall.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A simple blood test (25-hydroxyvitamin D) shows where you stand.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dose matters.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The commonly cited tolerable upper intake level for adults is 4,000 IU (100 mcg) a day, and taking much more for long stretches can raise blood calcium.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A vitamin supplement with D3, taken with a meal that contains fat, is a sensible starting point. Ask your doctor before going above the usual label dose.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Magnesium: a mineral many men miss</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Magnesium supports muscles, nerves and energy metabolism.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Based on NIH guidance, adults need 310 to 420 mg a day, and the upper limit for supplemental magnesium is 350 mg a day, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements'</span><a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/pubs/The_Scoop_May_2024.pdf"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">bone health bulletin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That limit applies only to supplements and medicines, not to magnesium in food. Too much from supplements can cause diarrhea.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Food sources such as nuts, seeds, beans and leafy greens do much of the work, and a small supplement dose can fill what is left.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Zinc deserves a quick mention here too.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NIH lists 40 mg a day as the adult upper limit, and very high doses can interfere with copper and magnesium absorption.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Most multivitamins already include zinc, so check labels before stacking products.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is not a steroid and will not build muscle on its own.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Creatine is also one of the best legal supplements for muscle growth, and it fits most strength training supplements stacks.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you have kidney disease or take medications that affect the kidneys, ask a doctor first.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Caffeine before training is another option, within your tolerance.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For the best supplement for muscle growth and the best workout supplements for men, those basics beat most flashy pre-workout blends.</span></p>
